## Read-only follow-up: trigger, HTTP outcomes and failure mechanism

The scheduler clock is `timed.timeOriginMs + timed.events[].atMs`; transport rows use independent integer `Date.now()` values in `timed.transport.rows[].startedAt/endedAt`. Their roughly4.6ms offset prevents sub-millisecond cross-clock ordering claims. Relative to A input13:29:23.4764Z: eligibility threshold5000ms; recorded eligible event5009.1ms; recorded `sync-start`5009.2ms; first actual GET request at13:29:28.481Z, approximately5004.6ms. That first request is approximately19995.9ms before Z input. Relative to Z input13:29:48.4769Z: eligibility threshold5000ms; recorded eligible event5001.3ms; durable ACK5035.8ms. There is no Z-specific sync dispatch or independently attributable Z upload request. The only dispatch began19991.3ms before Z input.

B–Y have their own immutable `eligibleAtMs = lastInput +5000`, but their recorded eligible events were batched at Z's idle tick while the first cloud operation remained in flight. The scheduler suppresses cloud wake timers during that flight; it does not emit or dispatch a new request for each page exactly at its five-second threshold. Their eligibility deadlines were not reset by later input. This distinction limits the claim that “every page automatically syncs at five seconds.”

The stopping mechanism is supported by [real-drive/store.mjs](../byos-sync/real-drive/store.mjs): its operation wrapper races initialization/preflight and the entire native sync callback against180000ms. The captured duration180002.6ms and absence of `sync-end` establish that this operation had not resolved before the deadline. They do **not** identify which internal await/subphase consumed it. The two conditional failures, request amplification, concurrent generations and provider latency are possible investigation leads, not demonstrated root causes.
